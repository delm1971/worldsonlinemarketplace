Will vs Trust: Which Estate Planning Tool Is Right for You?

A plain-English comparison of probate, upfront cost, privacy, and asset control—plus a decision checklist tailored to your life stage.

How Wills and Trusts Differ on Probate, Cost, Privacy & Control

Choosing between a last will and testament and a revocable living trust comes down to how much court oversight your family would face after you pass away and how much complexity you want to manage today.

Feature Last Will & Testament Revocable Living Trust
Probate Court Required (often takes 6–18+ months) Avoided for assets properly titled in the trust
Privacy Becomes a public court record Remains completely private
Upfront Cost Low ($100 – $500 online; $500+ attorney) Higher ($500 – $1,500+ online or attorney)
Control & Timing Takes effect only after death Active immediately; governs incapacity & staggered payouts
Naming Guardians Yes (the only way to nominate minor guardians) No (requires a companion pour-over will)

Decision Checklist by Life Stage

  • New Parents: Start with a Will at minimum so you can legally name guardians for minor children, and consider adding a trust if you want assets held until children reach age 25 or 30.
  • Homeowners: A Trust prevents your home from getting tied up in probate—especially critical if you own property in more than one state.
  • Small Business Owners & Side Hustlers: A Trust keeps LLC ownership transitions private and prevents business bank accounts from freezing during probate.
  • Single Adults with Simple Accounts: A Will combined with payable-on-death (POD) bank and retirement beneficiaries is fast, affordable, and straightforward.
